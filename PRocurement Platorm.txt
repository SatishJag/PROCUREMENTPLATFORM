The product should be designed as an end-to-end Source-to-Pay platform, not only a tendering application. It should connect procurement planning, sourcing, supplier management, contracts, purchasing, delivery, invoicing, project controls, and analytics in one auditable workflow.

## 2. Data-centre and construction procurement capabilities

These should be treated as a dedicated product domain rather than minor configuration:

- Project, program, site, building and data-hall hierarchy
- Procurement package register linked to WBS and cost codes
- BOQ, BOM and equipment schedule import from Excel
- Equipment specification and approved-equal management
- Long-lead equipment register
- Manufacturing and delivery milestone tracking
- Vendor document submittal register
- Technical submittal, material approval and shop-drawing linkage
- FAT, SAT, inspection and test-plan workflows
- Logistics, shipping, customs clearance and site-delivery tracking
- Capacity-based procurement analysis, including AED/MW and cost/MW
- Equipment tracking by make, model, serial number and asset tag
- Warranty, spare-parts and maintenance-document capture
- Commissioning and handover-document requirements
- Contractor, subcontractor, consultant and OEM relationship tracking
- Provisional sums, nominated suppliers and contractor procurement
- Retention, advance-payment guarantees, performance bonds and insurance
- Variation and claim linkage to contracts, budgets and forecasts
- Oracle budget, commitment and actual-cost reconciliation
- PMWeb/Procore package, schedule and project-control integration

## 3. Evaluation and award controls

The evaluation engine is one of the product’s most important differentiators.

It should support:

- Separate technical and commercial evaluation envelopes
- Blind evaluation where required
- Configurable weighted criteria and subcriteria
- Mandatory gates and minimum technical thresholds
- Individual evaluator scoring followed by consensus scoring
- Evaluation committees with quorum rules
- Evaluator conflict-of-interest declarations
- Comments and evidence required for low or high scores
- Automatic normalization and weighted-score calculation
- Price, quality, delivery, risk and total-cost-of-ownership models
- Bid deviation and qualification registers
- Alternative and value-engineering proposals
- Sensitivity and award-scenario modelling
- Split awards across suppliers, lots, regions or quantities
- Approval routing based on value, category, project and deviation
- Immutable evaluation and approval audit trail
- Automatically generated bid evaluation and award reports

## 4. AI and automation layer

AI should assist decisions while keeping procurement users accountable.

### Procurement copilot

- Convert a business request into a structured requisition
- Recommend procurement route, templates and timelines
- Generate draft scopes of work and RFx documents
- Suggest evaluation criteria
- Summarize bids, clarifications and supplier submissions
- Compare technical responses against requirements
- Identify missing information, exclusions and contradictory statements
- Draft clarification questions
- Prepare negotiation points and award recommendations
- Answer questions across contracts, bids, suppliers and POs

### Commercial intelligence

- Historical rate and price recommendations
- Quote coverage and confidence scoring
- Supplier recommendations based on capability and performance
- Bid outlier and collusion-risk indicators
- Cost-driver and should-cost analysis
- Currency and escalation impact modelling
- Total-cost-of-ownership analysis
- Demand aggregation opportunities

### Operational automation

- Document classification and metadata extraction
- Compliance-document expiry monitoring
- Approval and cycle-time bottleneck detection
- Invoice and PO exception triage
- Contract obligation extraction
- Risk-alert summarization
- Recommended next actions
- Automated reporting narratives

Every AI result should display its source evidence, confidence, model/version details where appropriate, and require human approval for controlled decisions.

## 5. Roles and portals

The platform should support distinct experiences for:

- Requester
- Project manager
- Procurement manager
- Buyer and sourcing specialist
- Category manager
- Technical evaluator
- Commercial evaluator/QS
- Legal and contract manager
- Finance and accounts payable
- Budget owner
- Executive approver
- HSE, QA/QC and compliance reviewer

## 6. Workflow and low-code configuration

Build a reusable workflow engine rather than hard-coding every process.

The administrator should be able to configure:

- Workflow stages and status transitions
- Sequential and parallel approvals
- Approval thresholds and authority matrices
- Conditional routing
- Delegation and out-of-office rules
- Escalation timeframes
- SLA timers
- Forms and custom fields
- Evaluation templates
- Document requirements
- Email and in-app notifications
- Business rules and validation
- Numbering formats
- Country, entity and project variations
- Workflow versioning and effective dates

## 8. Recommended technical architecture

Your proposed stack is suitable, with a few additions.

| Layer | Recommended technology |
|---|---|
| Web application | React with TypeScript, preferably Next.js |
| UI framework | Tailwind CSS with a reusable design system |
| Server state | TanStack Query |
| Client state | Zustand or Redux Toolkit where genuinely needed |
| Forms and validation | React Hook Form, Zod |
| Backend | Node.js with NestJS and TypeScript |
| API | REST/OpenAPI initially; event APIs for integrations |
| Transaction database | PostgreSQL |
| Cache | Redis |
| Search | OpenSearch or Azure AI Search |
| Workflow | Temporal, Camunda or a custom state-machine layer |
| Messaging | Azure Service Bus or Kafka |
| Documents | Azure Blob Storage with versioning and malware scanning |
| Identity | Microsoft Entra ID for employees; Entra External ID for suppliers |
| Analytics | Power BI semantic model and embedded dashboards |
| AI services | Azure OpenAI plus retrieval, evaluation and guardrail services |
| Observability | OpenTelemetry, Application Insights and centralized logs |
| Deployment | Azure Kubernetes Service or Azure Container Apps |
| CI/CD | Azure DevOps or GitHub Actions |
| Infrastructure | Terraform or Bicep |

A modular monolith is the sensible starting architecture. Clear domain modules can later be extracted into microservices when transaction volume, team ownership or scaling justifies it.

## 9. Key integrations

- Oracle Fusion: suppliers, budgets, PRs, POs, receipts, invoices, payments and chart of accounts
- PMWeb: projects, WBS, packages, contracts, commitments, progress and cost information
- Procore: project documents, submittals, RFIs and project procurement
- ProTenders and market-data services
- Microsoft 365: Outlook, Teams, SharePoint and Excel
- Power BI
- E-signature platforms
- Banking and supplier-verification services
- Tax, sanctions and corporate-registration services
- BIM/document-management platforms
- SSO and enterprise identity governance

Integration ownership must be explicit: each data object should have one authoritative system of record and defined synchronization rules.

## 10. Security and enterprise controls

Include these from the first release:

- Role- and attribute-based access control
- Segregation-of-duties rules
- Approval authority limits
- Project and legal-entity data isolation
- Supplier tenant isolation
- Encryption in transit and at rest
- Immutable audit events
- Field-level protection for bank and personal data
- Document access and download controls
- Malware scanning
- MFA and conditional access
- Data-retention and legal-hold policies
- API throttling and integration authentication
- Security monitoring and anomalous-access detection
- Backup, disaster recovery and tested restoration
- Configurable data residency
- AI prompt, response and evidence logging
- Controlled impersonation for support with full auditability

## 11. Important core data objects

The platform data model should include:

`Organization → Project → Budget → WBS → Procurement Plan → Package → Requisition → Sourcing Event → Lot → Bid → Evaluation → Award → Contract → PO → Shipment/Delivery → Receipt → Invoice → Payment`

Supporting master objects include suppliers, contacts, categories, items, cost codes, technical specifications, currencies, tax codes, locations, documents, workflows, approval matrices, risks, performance assessments and audit events.

## 12. Sensible delivery roadmap

### Phase 1 — Procurement foundation

- Identity and role management
- Supplier onboarding
- Guided intake
- Procurement planning
- RFx and tender management
- Technical/commercial evaluation
- Award approvals
- Audit trail
- Core dashboards
- Excel import/export

### Phase 2 — Contract and purchasing control

- Contract lifecycle management
- Obligations and securities
- Requisitions and POs
- Delivery and expediting
- Goods/service receipt
- Oracle integration
- Project and WBS integration

### Phase 3 — Supplier and commercial intelligence

- Supplier performance and risk
- Spend analytics
- Savings tracking
- Market intelligence
- Price benchmarks
- Long-lead equipment control
- Power BI analytics

### Phase 4 — AI-enabled procurement

- Guided intake copilot
- Document and bid summarization
- Compliance checking
- Supplier recommendations
- Bid comparison and anomaly detection
- Negotiation assistance
- Contract intelligence
- Predictive delays and risk alerts

The strongest initial product boundary is: **procurement planning through award, supplier onboarding, technical/commercial evaluation, contract handoff, and Oracle/PMWeb integration**. This provides a valuable first release without attempting to replace the ERP’s mature accounting and payment functions.
